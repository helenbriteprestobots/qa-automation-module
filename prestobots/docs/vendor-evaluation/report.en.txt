WHY WE DON'T USE AN OFF-THE-SHELF AI TESTING TOOL FOR THE CAUCE CHATBOT
========================================================================

Evaluation of Cekura, TestSprite and Momentic for end-to-end testing of the
Cauce WhatsApp chatbot (repo prestobotsv3, product Salud).
Date: 2026-11-19. Based on our trials and on each vendor's public
documentation as of this date.


1. SUMMARY
----------

These tools test what a product shows or says. The risk in our chatbot is
what it does behind the scenes: whether the appointment really ends up in
Macena and in our database.

None of the three tools can check that without us building the hard part
ourselves: sending messages through the bot's real channel, reading its
replies from Firestore, and verifying the booking in Macena. Once we build
that, the tool adds little on top, and it adds cost, vendor lock-in, and
patient data leaving our systems.

This isn't a criticism of the tools. They are good at what they're designed
for. Our product just has a different shape.

  * Momentic
      Designed for: clicking through web pages and mobile apps.
      Main gap:     the bot has no screen to click. No WhatsApp support.

  * TestSprite
      Designed for: web UIs and REST APIs that answer each request.
      Main gap:     our webhook always answers "received", so every test
                    would look like a pass.

  * Cekura
      Designed for: conversations with voice and chat agents.
      Main gap:     it judges what the bot says, not what it does. No
                    buttons, lists or WhatsApp Flows in its docs.


2. WHAT TESTING OUR CHATBOT REQUIRES
------------------------------------

The Cauce chatbot doesn't live on a web page. A patient writes on WhatsApp,
the message travels through Gupshup to our servers, Dialogflow CX interprets
it, and the bot books the appointment in Macena (the clinics' booking
system).

To test one booking from start to finish, a test must:

  1. Send a message as a patient. It goes through the Gupshup webhook (the
     address where the bot receives WhatsApp messages) or as a real WhatsApp
     message.

  2. Get the bot's reply. The webhook only answers "received". The bot works
     in the background and saves its reply in Firestore, so the test has to
     read the database to see it.

  3. Handle WhatsApp's formats. The bot answers with buttons, lists, and
     WhatsApp Flows (the in-chat booking forms).

  4. Check what actually happened. Was the appointment created in Macena?
     Was it saved in Firestore?

  5. Judge the replies in Spanish. Replies are natural language, so exact
     text matching breaks too easily. An AI judge has to grade them.

Points 2 and 4 are where most of the risk is, and no tool covers them.


3. TOOL BY TOOL
---------------

3.1 Momentic - built for clicking through web pages and mobile apps

  - It drives Chromium browsers, iOS simulators and Android emulators. Its
    AI features (selectors that repair themselves, visual checks) need a
    screen to click. The bot has no screen.

  - It has no WhatsApp or chatbot support. Its SMS inbox isn't WhatsApp.

  - It can be forced to work: an HTTP step posts to the webhook and a
    JavaScript step reads Firestore. But then we'd be writing our own test
    harness inside someone else's sandbox, paying for AI features we don't
    use.

  Verdict: wrong kind of tool for the bot. It could be considered later for
  the admin dashboard or the marketing landing page, which do have screens.


3.2 TestSprite - built for web UIs and REST APIs

  - Its backend tests call an endpoint and check the response. Our webhook
    always returns "200 received", whatever the bot does afterwards.
    TestSprite would mark every test as passed, even when the bot answered
    nonsense or didn't answer at all.

  - Its documentation doesn't mention asynchronous or event-driven flows,
    database checks, or chatbots.

  - It writes test cases by reading the code and the app's URL. For us it
    would propose tests for the API endpoints, not patient conversations
    based on the Dialogflow CX agent.

  - When we reviewed it, its GitHub integration only re-ran existing tests.
    It didn't write new ones when the code changed.

  Verdict: its request -> response model doesn't fit a bot that answers
  asynchronously through another channel.


3.3 Cekura - the closest fit, but it judges words, not results

  Cekura is built for conversational agents. It has WhatsApp text testing,
  simulated users, and AI-graded metrics. It's the only one of the three
  that understands "conversations". Even so:

  - It needs a real WhatsApp line. It sends real WhatsApp messages from its
    own number to the bot's number (its setup guide asks for Twilio
    credentials). Our stage bot would need a real number reachable from
    outside, and WhatsApp's 24-hour messaging window applies.

  - Text only. Its WhatsApp docs only describe text. They don't mention
    buttons, lists or Flows, and the Cauce booking path depends on them.

  - It doesn't check results. It grades the conversation transcript, and its
    docs show no way to check Firestore or Macena. A bot that says "your
    appointment is confirmed" without creating it in Macena would pass.
    That is the worst bug this bot can have.

  - Scenarios are written by hand. Test cases are prompts that someone
    writes. It doesn't find them in the Dialogflow CX agent or in the Flow
    files.

  - The workaround is most of the work. It can connect through a WebSocket
    address that we host. But we'd have to build a bridge between Cekura,
    the Gupshup webhook and Firestore, which is most of the work of testing
    the bot anyway.

  Verdict: useful for judging conversation quality, but blind to whether the
  booking really happened.


4. PROBLEMS SHARED BY ALL THREE
-------------------------------

  - Patient data leaves our systems. Test conversations include health data
    and would be stored by a third-party SaaS.

  - They can't tell our bugs from other people's outages. None of them
    distinguishes a failure in our bot from Gupshup being slow or Macena's
    test system being down. Every outage would look like a bot bug.

  - Lock-in and cost. Tests live on the vendor's platform, and we pay per
    seat or per run.

  - The hard part still falls on us. The channel integration, reading
    Firestore, and verifying Macena have to be built by us in every case.


5. WHAT WE'RE DOING INSTEAD
---------------------------

We're building our own testing platform on proven open-source pieces (see
docs/04-e2e-testing-strategy.md and docs/06-mvp-scope.md):

  - A runner that plays the patient by sending messages to the real webhook
    on salud-stage (never production).

  - It reads the bot's replies from Firestore with read-only access.

  - It checks the booking in Macena's test system.

  - An AI judge (Claude) grades the Spanish replies, but it can never turn a
    failed check into a pass.

  - Test cases are proposed automatically from the Dialogflow CX agent and
    the Flow files, and approved by an admin.

  - Failures become Jira cards.

The pass/fail decision rests on yes/no checks (did the reply arrive, are the
right buttons there, does the appointment exist). The AI only grades what
can't be checked exactly.
